Milestone 34 Review & M35 Plan

Sprint ran 20 October – 2 November 2026 · Next sprint M35, 3 – 16 November · Team of 3 · Prepared for the CEO & COO · 3 November 2026

The release happened. Version 1.91.0 went to production on the evening of 2 November: every change merged since 25 September, 105 of them, after 38 days. Billing is now live inside Konvoy Cloud for the whole Konvoy team, reading any NetSuite-linked customer’s invoices and credit notes; the exclusive-agreement changes Tommy and Alice asked for are in; the new Transfers page and its flow chart are on for every Konvoy staff member; and the LaunchDarkly add-on that was sending login passwords to a third party, flagged in the last two reports, is finally gone from production. The team was told in #konvoyteam that night. Nothing new has appeared in the error tracker since.

It was also the strongest fortnight of the quarter. Milestone 34 closed at 79% (86 of 109), against 67% last time, and behind it 73 issues closed and 63 pull requests merged, plus 14 infrastructure changes. Bea took the customer dashboard from one piece to twelve of nineteen. Millicent finished the PHP 8.5 branch and, on the way, did nine of the twelve test-quality fixes that last report listed as parked. Finance’s whole Billing list was closed, the entire team tested it on the test environment, and Patrick gave the go on 30 October. A critical PHP vulnerability published on 22 October was patched in production the same day as a stand-alone hotfix.

What did not move: the four access findings and Kubernetes. Two of the findings sat in the milestone all sprint and were not touched; a fourth was found on 22 October. Kubernetes 1.35 now has 29 days to the 2 December deadline and has had no work for three sprints. The plan for M35, in order: the follow-through Patrick wants — Billing switched on for customers in about two weeks, which needs three decisions below — then PHP 8.5 onto the main branch and released on its own, then the last seven dashboard pieces. Kubernetes and the access findings need a yes or a no today, not another carry.

Released · v1.91.0 · 2 November

Two production releases this sprint, after none in M32 and M33. The hotfix v1.90.3 on 22 October carried only the PHP security patch, built on top of the old release so the 100 waiting changes did not have to go out with it. Then v1.91.0 on 2 November carried everything: 104 pull requests, 33 of them features, one additive database change, no downtime.

Live now: staff Billing with every Finance follow-up; agreement delete, the ended-extension correction and pre-filled end dates; the Transfers schedule confirmation and flow view; the “The” search fix; the spreadsheet-ready Rentals download; the AU surcharge clause removed; fuel-levy rates for Konvoy ANZ; the Google Maps blank-screen fix; the second-account identity bug; staff instruction guides; and the LaunchDarkly leak fix and add-on removal. Dark behind their switches: the customer dashboard and forecast page, and the customer-facing Billing page. PHP 8.5 is not in this release; it ships separately, next.

What we finished

Six themes, in the order a manager should care about them. Underlined names link to the GitHub record. “Live” means in production; “Switched off” means in production but dark behind its switch; “Branch” means merged to a branch, not yet on main.

1. Billing is live for the Konvoy team Live

Mariana’s seven asks from the 15 October audit were all merged by 25 October: search by invoice, credit-note or order number; filters that look across the whole ledger rather than the page on screen; totals for whatever is filtered, per currency; New Zealand amounts shown as NZD$; downloads named by document number; a column chooser; and a note on how fresh the figures are. On 27 October the page was opened to the whole team on the test environment with the twelve real customers. Mariana: “a great addition to KC”. Alice checked several NZ customers against NetSuite and “can’t find any discrepancies”. Grant: “clean and simple to use”. Patrick on 30 October: comments by end of day, then production, then customers after a couple of weeks (the Billing epic).

In production since 2 November it reads any NetSuite-linked Australian or New Zealand customer, not only the twelve, and the Email button sends the PDF to the customer for real — Konvoy admins only, after a confirmation. Three things it does not do yet, all stated in the team announcement: customers cannot see their own Billing page (switched off); a customer in the EU or NA region shows an error rather than an empty list, because the read-only NetSuite credential only exists for Australia; and a customer who calls the API directly can read their own invoices today even with the page switched off, which was a deliberate design choice but deserves a product or security decision before the customer rollout.

2. Two releases: a same-day security hotfix, then the big one Live

On 22 October a vulnerability in PHP itself was published that allowed unauthenticated remote code execution. The platform was patched in production that day as v1.90.3, a hotfix built on top of the September release carrying only the patch and the dependency updates its safety gate required — the first time the team has shipped a hotfix this way rather than releasing everything on the main branch to get one fix out (the PHP 8.4.27 hotfix). The procedure now needs writing down, and the hotfix branch deleting.

v1.91.0 followed on 2 November once Finance’s list was closed and Patrick had given the go. The release preparation was done properly: the notes reconciled against every pull request since September, every pull request labelled, the ten production feature switches audited one by one, the test-environment and pre-production deployments proven green on the exact commit being tagged, and the three production regions deployed and checked. Two things were found on the way and fixed before the tag: the test-environment frontend had silently not deployed since 27 October (watch item 5), and the signed billing-PDF links needed keeping out of the web-server logs in all three regions.

Why it matters: 38 days of work reached customers in one clean step, a critical vulnerability was closed within hours instead of waiting for that step, and the password-leak fix the last two reports asked for is finally in production.

3. The customer dashboard: 1 of 19 to 12 of 19 in a fortnight Switched off

Bea’s sprint. The customer portal now has its shell — top bar, organisation card, account-manager card, and header actions for a new order, a forecast update and a keg collection — and the first real tiles: month- and year-to-date rentals, the next order, the exclusive-contract band, and a rental-volume chart by keg size against forecast. The Forecast page was rebuilt to the v2.2 design with a five-month grid, “add” cells and copy-last-year, and locked months can no longer be overwritten by a late draft (the customer portal epic). Rob built the backend summaries the remaining tiles need — account standing and on-hold state for the overdue-invoices tile, customer rates, and per-status rental counts — and answered the design questions that were blocking them (freight is per keg; the Rentals total is ex-GST, all billed lines, fuel levy included).

Seven pieces remain and five are already in review: the rates card (approved), the overdue-invoices tile, the customer Rentals page, the Forecast page’s missing-months banner, and a provisional pill on the contract band. Eight small v2.2 follow-ups were raised on 1–2 November from Rob’s reviews. One trap found during the release audit: the dashboard’s switch is set so that turning it on would show it to every customer at once; that default is to be changed before any rollout.

Why it matters: this is Patrick’s priority #2 after Billing, and it went from “started” to “two weeks from a first look” in one sprint.

4. PHP 8.5 finished on its branch, and the parked test-quality work done with it Branch

Millicent’s sprint. The upgrade epic is at 20 of 22 pieces, all on its own branch, verified with a clean from-scratch rebuild on 28 October. What the last report listed as parked — the test-quality remediation epic, “closed with none of its twelve pieces started” — was quietly done on the same branch: nine of the twelve, every backend one. Tests that could not fail now can; tests that checked a copy of production now check production code; mutation testing has a baseline for billing, keg replay and sign-in; and controller access checks were moved to one declarative pattern (the PHP 8.5 epic, the test-quality epic). The three frontend test pieces remain.

The merge to the main branch has been open as a draft since 26 October, deliberately held until v1.91.0 was out so the two could not be confused. That condition is now met. One detail carried from the hotfix: the branch must land with the 8.5.12 floor, because the same vulnerability exists in older 8.5 builds.

Why it matters: the runtime upgrade is a fortnight of careful work done early rather than a crisis later, and the test suite that guards every release got materially harder to fool.

5. Exclusive agreements, and the fixes customers and support asked for Live

Konvoy admins can now remove an agreement entered by mistake, with a reason, a Slack note to Finance and a restore path — Alice’s request from the feedback widget on 20 October, live thirteen days later (agreement delete). The ended-extension counting was corrected to Tommy’s reading — the whole contract’s volume, start to extension end — which reverses the guidance in the 15 September update, and the end date is pre-filled. Both were called out to Sales in the announcement because they change figures Sales watches.

Also live: venue searches beginning with “The” work (Alice); the Rentals download is one row per keg size, ready for a pivot table (Alice); the credit-card surcharge clause is out of the AU agreement (Mariana); Konvoy ANZ can see fuel-levy rates (Feruz); a Google Maps failure no longer blanks the app; customers no longer see refill orders in their Orders report; and the second-account identity bug — on its fourth report as “parked” — turned out to be fixed by the sign-in change and was closed on 27 October. A slow sign-in query that was tripping the error tracker was fixed too.

Why it matters: every one of these was a question someone in the business had asked, and several came in through the feedback widget and shipped inside the same sprint.

6. Transfers: on for all Konvoy staff, and Alice wants the same chart on Rentals Live

The updated Transfers page and its Flow view of where kegs go, which last report said reached only two people, was switched on for every @konvoykegs.com user with the release, and the user guide for it is published. Scheduling a transfer now says exactly who will be emailed, which closed Marc’s “mystery email” question. After trying the Flow view on the test environment, Alice asked on 29 October for the same picture on the Rentals page — NZ often supplies customers from a warehouse that is not their local one, and she wants to see it. Why it matters: the ops team now sees keg movement in production, not just on the test environment, and the first request for more of it arrived unprompted.

Five things to watch

Ordered by how quickly they need a decision.

1. Four access findings in production, two sprints without an hour of work

Last report asked for the three findings to be sequenced ahead of dashboard work. Two went into the milestone, neither was touched, and a fourth — another API with no authorisation check, on keg collections — was found by Millicent on 22 October. The dashboard moved eleven pieces in the same fortnight. That is a choice the sprint made by default, and it should be made on purpose.

Decision: either the two authorisation gaps go first in M35, before any dashboard follow-up, or we record that they wait until the dashboard is in customers’ hands. Either is defensible; the default is not.

2. Kubernetes 1.35 — 29 days, three sprints of no movement

Standard support for the current version ends on 2 December. After that AWS charges the $2,147 AUD a month Extended Support surcharge removed in August, roughly $26,000 a year, until all four clusters are upgraded. Last report said the work fits if it starts in M34; it did not start. It no longer fits beside the Billing customer rollout and the Rentals chart in M35, which are both Rob’s.

Decision: yes means Rob does the upgrade in M35 and the Rentals chart and the hotfix tidy-up wait; no means budgeting about $2,150 AUD a month from December until it is done. One or the other today.

3. The customer Billing rollout needs three answers that are not Engineering’s

Patrick wants customers on Billing in about two weeks. The page is built and tested, but three things are still open that only the business can close: whether customer-facing documents show the receivership wording in the legal-entity name (asked in September); whether a customer should be able to read their invoices through the API while the page is switched off (they can today, by design); and whether EU and NA customers get connected or get a clean “not available” message. The UAT sign-off is also still a Slack thread rather than a line on the ticket.

Decision: Patrick answers the wording and the API question this week; Rob proposes a first customer list; Bea records the sign-off on the ticket from the 27–30 October thread.

4. Sign-ups are failing to write bank details to NetSuite, and Finance is covering it by hand

Since August, five new customers’ bank details have failed to save into NetSuite at sign-up. Konvoy Cloud keeps the sign-up and alerts Finance, who enter the details manually. The cause is a permission inside NetSuite’s UK localisation add-on, not Konvoy Cloud code, so Engineering cannot fix it alone.

What would help: a NetSuite administrator (or the NetSuite partner) grants the integration role access to the payment-file-format record. Half a day for whoever has admin; nothing to build.

5. The deploy alert that slipped twice just cost a day

Two reports ago the test environment had deployed silently red for a month; the ask was a Slack alert when a deploy stops. It was not built in M33 or M34. On 1 November, preparing the release, Rob found the test-environment frontend had silently not deployed since 27 October — a low-severity dependency advisory had been quietly skipping every deploy. It was fixed before the tag, but it was found by inspection rather than by an alert, and the pre-production tests had been running against a stale frontend for five days without anyone knowing.

Nothing from you: it is a day’s work and it goes into M35 behind row 1. Flagging it because it is the third time it appears here and the first time it has had a visible cost.
