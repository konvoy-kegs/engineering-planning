AWS Extended Support Exit

Progress page · RDS MySQL 8.4 and EKS 1.34 · Last updated 17 August 2026

Two AWS surcharges were about to run indefinitely, and both have now been dealt with. AWS charges an Extended Support premium once a database engine or Kubernetes version falls out of standard support. The charge is automatic and open-ended, and it buys nothing — you pay a premium to keep running the older version.

The key point about timing: no action is required to be charged. Action is required to avoid being charged. That is why both pieces of work were urgent rather than merely worthwhile — every day of delay was billable, and neither meter would ever stop on its own.

The money

Two different kinds of money. They should not be added together without saying which is which.
$2,147
AUD / month — removed
EKS. This was already being charged. It is now gone from the bill — measured at $0.00 on 10 August, not projected.
$1,896
AUD / month — avoided
RDS. The meter started 1 August. The upgrade landed before it accumulated, so this never reached the bill. Nothing to see in a cost report.
$48.5k
AUD / year — combined
Roughly $4,043/month across both, on a run-rate basis, provided the versions stay current.
Read the two figures differently. The EKS $2,147 is a genuine reduction — a line that appeared on the AWS bill and no longer does. The RDS $1,896 is avoided cost: it was never paid, so no invoice will ever show it falling. Both are real, but only one of them will be visible as a saving in a finance report, and presenting them as a single “saving” figure would misrepresent the second.

Why these were urgent

Both were deadline-driven by AWS, not by us.

RDS — the meter started on 1 August

MySQL 8.0 left standard support on 31 July 2026. From 1 August, Extended Support billed per vCPU-hour on rds-francis — 18 chargeable vCPU, about $1,895.58 AUD/month, indefinitely, for no benefit. MySQL 8.4 is in standard support until 31 July 2029.

Why it could not wait: the charge had already begun. Each week of delay was roughly $440 for nothing.

EKS — already inside the surcharge

All four clusters were on Kubernetes 1.33 and already inside Extended Support, actively paying $2,147 AUD/month. Moving to 1.34 exits the surcharge. The upgrade also had to be sequenced carefully — four clusters, addon version dependencies, and a soak gate on dev before any production hop.

Why it could not wait: unlike the RDS case this was money already going out the door every month, so the payback on the work was immediate.

What landed 3 – 14 August

37 items closed across the terraform and kubernetes repositories during the Milestone 24 window. None of it appears in the Milestone 24 issue counts, which cover the konvoy repository only.

EKS: all four clusters upgraded and the charge verified gone Done

The clusters ran 1.33 → 1.34 on 8–9 August, in order, with a soak gate on dev-oc before any production hop (terraform #589 dev-oc, #591 prod-oc, #592 prod-eu, #593 prod-na). Preparation work went in first: control-plane audit logging, a CI gate on the three production apply jobs, managed addon pins stepped ahead of the control plane, and audits of kube-proxy mode, CoreDNS versions and PodDisruptionBudgets. On 10 August #594 confirmed the extendedSupport billing line at $0.00, with a control line proving the clusters were still running. Epic #573 closed the same day.

Result: $2,147 AUD/month off the bill, verified by measurement rather than assumed. cluster_support_type = "STANDARD" is now declared in code, so the billable default cannot silently return.

RDS: the upgrade closed out and the refresh pipeline repaired Done

The production database upgrade itself ran on 2 August — just before this window, and covered in the Milestone 23 review. What landed in this sprint was the close-out: epic terraform #523 closed on 7 August, and four defects in the database refresh pipeline were fixed — a hardcoded serverVersion=8.0 (#526), the pipeline copying the live database's configuration (#534), a security-group conflict (#556), and an unusable snapshot override (#568). Backup retention on production was cut 35 → 14 days (#602).

Why the pipeline work mattered: the refresh pipeline is how non-production environments get realistic data. It had been quietly producing 8.0-shaped clones, which would have reintroduced the old engine version by the back door.

A guardrail so this cannot recur Done

The root problem was not the upgrade — it was that nobody was watching the calendar. A monthly support-lifecycle watch now runs across both EKS and RDS (terraform #575), checking every cluster and engine against its standard-support end date. It warns at 120 days for EKS and 180 days for RDS, and files an issue automatically.

It has already fired. On its first run it filed #649, naming all four clusters 112 days ahead of the next deadline — which is how we know about December rather than finding out from an invoice.

The next deadline

This is the one thing on this page that needs a decision rather than an update.
2 December 2026
Kubernetes 1.34 leaves standard support

On that date the $2,147 AUD/month surcharge returns in full on all four clusters unless they are on 1.35. The saving just banked is a fixed-term one: it lasts until December, and then it has to be earned again.

December needs 1.35 only — it does not need 1.36. That distinction matters, because treating the two as one job is what previously made this look unachievable. The 1.36 upgrade (terraform #597) is gated behind migrating off ingress-nginx (#601), and both are 2027 work.

The 1.35 upgrade (#596) is currently Blocked with a target of 18 November 2026 — two weeks of margin against a hard billing date.
