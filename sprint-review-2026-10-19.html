Milestone 33 Review & M34 Plan

Sprint ran 7 – 19 October 2026 · Next sprint M34, 20 October – 2 November · Team of 3 · Prepared for the CEO & COO · 19 October 2026

Billing is in Finance’s hands. On 7 October the new Billing page was switched on in the test environment for twelve real customers — seven Australian, five New Zealand — reading their invoices and credit notes live from production NetSuite, read-only. Patrick found the first defect within hours and it was fixed the same day; his verdict was “the functionality looks good”. Mariana’s audit on 15 October came back with seven asks, all sensible, none structural: three are in review today, four are queued. The updated Transfers page went into testing alongside it, with the flow chart Marc asked for on 7 October built two days later.

The numbers are softer than last time, for a good reason. Milestone 33 closed at 67% (38 of 57) against 90% for the extended M32. Of the 19 items that carry, eleven are Billing follow-ups from Finance’s testing and the Transfers documentation, all raised in the last week of the sprint — that is scope arriving, not work slipping. Behind the milestone, 48 issues closed and 31 pull requests merged, plus 13 infrastructure changes. Millicent took the PHP 8.5 upgrade to 12 of 13 pieces. The Databricks board closed on 15 October and the database board is down to its last item.

The honest part is unchanged from last report: there has been no release. The last one was 25 September, 24 days ago. Fifty-five changes are waiting, including the fix for the LaunchDarkly add-on that was sending login passwords to a third party, which last report asked to be the first thing done in this sprint. The plan for M34, in order: finish Mariana’s Billing list, cut the release, bring PHP 8.5 onto the main branch for testing, then start the customer dashboard. The Kubernetes decision also needs a yes today.

Still not released · 24 days

v1.90.2 on 25 September remains the last production release. Nothing merged in M32 or M33 has reached customers. The main branch now carries 55 changes waiting to go out.

Waiting: the whole Billing epic and this sprint’s Billing fixes; the LaunchDarkly recording fix and add-on removal; the API documentation migration; the Transfers flow view; the Notifications error for operator Managers; the Google Maps failure that blanked the whole app; fuel-levy rate access for Konvoy ANZ staff; the sign-in error straight after a token refresh; the credit-card surcharge clause removed from the Australian sign-up agreement; and the customer dashboard shell, dark behind its switch. PHP 8.5 is not in this list — it is still on its own branch and should be released separately, after the above.

What we finished

Six themes, in the order a manager should care about them. Underlined names link to the GitHub record. “Live” means in production; “Merged” means waiting for the next release; “Testing” means on the test environment.

1. Billing: twelve customers’ real invoices on the test environment, Finance auditing them Testing

The step last report called the hard one — making the test environment behave as if it were connected to NetSuite — is done, and done the safe way. For twelve named customers the Billing page reads production NetSuite through the read-only login; every other customer shows an empty list; nothing on the test environment can write to NetSuite, and the writer refuses to start if it is ever pointed at production outside production. Finance got a short walkthrough video from Bea, a Billing section in the user guide, and an open invitation to compare Konvoy Cloud against NetSuite customer by customer (the Billing epic).

Patrick found the first defect within hours: Moon Dog’s credit notes did not appear when he filtered to credit notes. The filter had only been looking at the 25 documents on screen; it now asks NetSuite for exactly the type requested, and the count at the top matches. Fixed and on the test environment the same day. Mariana’s audit on 15 October then produced the list below. Her closing line was “other than this all looks good — I love that you can download invoices directly from here.”

Still to do before a customer sees it: finish the list above; written sign-off from Patrick and from Mariana or Rose on the UAT ticket (today it is a Slack thread); Patrick’s answer on the legal-entity wording, since Konvoy Services’ name includes “(Receivers and Managers Appointed)”; the release; then switch it on for the twelve. The production email plumbing that was the last infrastructure prerequisite was finished on 12 October.

Why it matters: this is the first time anyone outside Engineering has used the page against real money, and the verdict is “looks good, make it easier to find things” — which is the best possible kind of feedback.

2. Transfers: the updated page is in testing, with the flow chart Marc asked for Testing

The new Transfers page shares its machinery with the new Rentals page — the same filters, column picker, email-status indicator and chart — and is live on the test environment behind its switch. Marc’s review on 7 October asked for one thing it did not have: a picture of where kegs come from and where they go. Two days later the chart card gained a Flow view: every warehouse on the left, every destination on the right, the width of each band the number of kegs, with the page’s own filters narrowing it and a Locations-or-States grouping (the transfer flow chart). Rob’s review turned it from a second card into a tab on the existing one. The user guide for the updated page and the Flow view is written; its screenshots are being recaptured from a fictional set of transfers so no real customer appears in the documentation.

Marc’s other point — that creating a transfer “sends it off straight away” — was run to ground. It does not: saving a transfer emails nobody outside Konvoy; Schedule is what sends, to the warehouse and the transport provider, immediately. The confusion was that nothing told you so, so the Schedule confirmation now lists exactly who will be emailed. Two follow-ups are raised (filter the Flow view by SKU; click a SKU in the chart legend to isolate it) and a preliminary investigation into suggesting the right transport provider from history is written up for discussion, not approved — it needs Marc’s and Kim’s answers on whether sending should be timed to the transfer date at all.

Why it matters: the ops team gets the picture of keg movement they have been assembling by hand, and a “mystery email” turns out to be a labelling problem rather than a defect. In production the new page still reaches only two people until its switch is ramped — watch item 5.

3. PHP 8.5 upgrade: 12 of 13 pieces done, on its own branch Branch

Millicent’s sprint. The platform’s language runtime was bumped to PHP 8.5 with every dependency pinned and the test suite green, then nine remediation pieces landed one by one — deprecations swept, rounding made explicit, input validation made strict, the test framework moved to PHPUnit 13 — and a final pass removed about 3,400 lines of duplicated tests that were checking the same thing twice (the PHP 8.5 epic). The one piece left is the merge to the main branch, which M34 plans deliberately after the release so the two cannot be confused with each other.

Why it matters: staying on a current runtime is what keeps security patches available and upgrades small; done now, it is a fortnight of careful work rather than a crisis later.

4. API documentation now generated from the code — and breaking changes are blocked Epic closed 15 Oct

Last report had this at 10 of 14. The remaining endpoint groups were migrated, the old hand-written tooling removed, and the whole thing merged to the main branch on 15 October — roughly 9,900 lines in, 8,600 out (API docs migration). Every backend change now shows reviewers precisely what it alters in the published contract, and a change that would break an existing integration fails the build rather than reaching a partner.

Why it matters: partners integrating with the API get documentation that is always right, and we cannot break them by accident.

5. The customer dashboard — Patrick’s priority #2 — has started 1 of 18

On 14 October the customer portal work was written up as an epic of 18 pieces: a portal shell with the customer’s organisation and account manager, tiles for overdue invoices and account standing, month- and year-to-date rentals, the next order, rental and freight rates, a rental-volume chart against forecast, and updated Rentals and Forecast pages (the customer portal epic). The first piece — the dashboard page itself, dark behind a switch — merged on 15 October. The overdue-invoices tile is where Billing and the dashboard meet, which is why Billing had to come first.

Why it matters: this is the customer-facing work Patrick sequenced after Billing in August; it starts in earnest in M34 once the Billing list and the release are out of the way.

6. Fixes customers and support will notice, and the staff instruction guides Merged

A failure loading Google Maps used to blank the entire application; now only the map is affected. Konvoy ANZ staff can see fuel-levy rates including history, which was raised through the feedback widget. A sign-in error that appeared right after a successful token refresh is fixed, as is the Notifications error for operator Managers carried from M32. The credit-card surcharge clause was removed from the Australian sign-up agreement to match the new rule. And the staff-only instruction guides — how-to documents inside Konvoy Cloud for support staff, with click-to-copy identifiers and drawn figures — are on the test environment; file attachments were dropped as not needed, and the gate proving only Konvoy staff can see them was tested rather than assumed.

Why it matters: every one of these is a support conversation that stops happening — once released.

Six things to watch

Ordered by how quickly they need a decision.

1. No release in 24 days — the password-leak fix is still not in production

Last report asked for a release in the first week of M33 so the LaunchDarkly fix would go live. It did not happen; the sprint went to the Billing rollout instead. Production is still running code from 25 September, and the add-on that sent login passwords and reset tokens to a third party is still running with it.

Decision: a named day this week. The plan above puts it second, behind the three Billing fixes already in review; if those slip, release without them.

2. Billing sign-off is a Slack thread, not a signature

Patrick’s “looks good” and Mariana’s nine asks are the best evidence the page is right, and none of it is recorded on the UAT ticket. The entity-wording question — whether customers see “(Receivers and Managers Appointed)” — has been waiting for Patrick since early September and is the one item on the list that Engineering cannot answer.

Decision: Patrick and Mariana (or Rose) write their sign-off on the UAT ticket once the three in-review fixes are on the test environment; Patrick answers the wording this week.

3. Three access findings in production, none scheduled

Accounts that are not Konvoy staff hold the global maintainer role in the Australian production system; the rolling-collections schedule API performs no authorisation check, so a logged-in user could likely read or change another customer’s schedule; and the fuel-surcharge rates API lets a default-tenant user read any organisation’s rates. The first two sat in M33 unassigned to a date; the third was found this sprint. None is known to have been exploited.

Decision: put all three in M34 ahead of dashboard work, owner named today.

4. Kubernetes 1.35 — six weeks left and two sprints of no movement

Our Kubernetes version leaves AWS standard support on 2 December. After that AWS either upgrades part of it for us, leaving the rest behind, or the Extended Support surcharge — the $2,147 AUD a month removed in August — comes back. The work still fits if it starts in M34; it will not fit if it starts in M35.

Decision: yes or no today. A no means budgeting the surcharge from December.

5. The new Transfers page reaches two people in production

Everything in theme 2 is live on the test environment, but in production the switch for the new Transfers page is on for two named users only. Marc will not see the flow chart in his daily work until it is ramped, and ramping it is a product decision that was parked in September.

Decision: who tests on the test environment and by when, then a ramp date after the release.

6. Housekeeping that slipped a second time, so it is not a surprise

The Slack alert for failed deploys asked for last time was not built — the test environment has deployed cleanly since 6 October, but nothing would tell us if it stopped. The test-quality remediation epic is still closed with none of its twelve pieces started, and the two parked customer defects are on their fifth and fourth report.

Nothing from you unless you want them scheduled — in which case the deploy alert is a day and the two defects fit in M34 behind the four priorities. Otherwise they are parked, and this is the last time they appear here until something changes.
