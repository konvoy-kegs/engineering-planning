Sprint Hygiene Runbook

The 90-minutes-per-week operating system for keeping the Katch Cloud sprint board, retro inputs, and team workload coherent. Built for a 3-person team today; designed to scale unchanged when the team grows. Tick boxes save locally — your progress survives a refresh.

Goal: start the week with an accurate board, named owners on everything In-Progress, and a known plan for the next 48 hours. ≈30 min.

1. Board reset KEEP

▼
Open the Kanban view → Project board
Walk the board right-to-left (Done → QA → Review → In-Progress → Ready). Right-to-left forces you to clear the end of the pipeline first.
For every card in Review older than 3 days: comment, ping the author, or merge. Do not let it sit a second Monday.
Exit criteria: Every In-Progress item has an assignee, size, and milestone. No Review card > 3 days without an action today.
Why right-to-left?

Left-to-right is how work flows; right-to-left is how problems are discovered. State accumulates at the end of the pipeline. If you start at Backlog you'll plan new work and never notice 14 items rotting in Review (Sprint 2's actual peak).

2. WIP check KEEP

▼
Count In-Progress items per assignee. Target: ≤ 2 per dev.
Count Review items overall. Target: ≤ 5. (Sprint 2 peaked at 14 — that's the bug this catches.)
If Review > 5, block a 60-min Friday review slot on your calendar now.
gh project item-list 4 --owner konvoy-kegs --format json \ | jq '[.items[] | select(.status == "In Progress")] | group_by(.assignees[0].login) | map({owner: .[0].assignees[0].login, count: length})'
Exit criteria: WIP per person ≤ 2, Review ≤ 5, or remediation booked.

3. Sprint goal pulse KEEP

▼
Re-read the sprint goal in the latest planning dashboard (published index).
For each goal, identify the next 1–2 tickets that must move this week. Comment "Priority for this week — please pick up next" on those tickets.
If a goal is visibly off-track at Day 3, name it. Don't let it die quietly.
Exit criteria: Each sprint goal has a named next ticket and a named owner.

4. Blockers & dependencies KEEP

▼
Filter the board for the blocked label.
For each blocked item: identify the blocker, the owner of the blocker, and an unblock action. Comment on the ticket.
Cross-check the dependency map in the latest planning dashboard for anything you've forgotten.
Exit criteria: Zero unowned blockers.

5. Sentry & Grafana scan DELEGATE → on-call

▼
Sentry — sort by "new this week". Triage anything P1/P2 into the board with a sprint label, or backlog if it can wait.
Grafana — open API latency and error-rate boards. Anything red or trending? File a ticket.
Exit criteria: No untriaged P1/P2 Sentry issue from the last 7 days.

6. Standup async post KEEP

▼
Drop a 4-line Monday post in #eng-katch-cloud: sprint day number, today's goal-of-the-week per dev, anything blocked, what you're unblocking today.
Exit criteria: Post is up before the team logs on.
Goal: catch scope drift and review bloat before it eats Friday. Wednesday is your last chance to course-correct without burning the weekend. ≈20 min.

1. Burn-up vs trajectory KEEP

▼
Open Insights → Burn up.
Eyeball the line. By Wednesday of week 2 you should be ~70% Done. Under 50% = sprint in trouble. Call it now, not Friday.
Exit criteria: Trajectory looks healthy, OR a "we're not going to finish X" message has gone to CEO/COO.

2. Scope creep audit KEEP

▼
Filter board for items added this sprint (created date inside the sprint window).
Compare against the planned scope (in the planning dashboard).
Anything added that isn't a P0 bug should be backed out, deferred, or explicitly traded for a planned item that gets dropped.
Why this step exists

Sprint 2 pulled in 80+ SP of unplanned work against a 24 SP plan. The team finished sprint 2 thinking they delivered a lot — they did — but none of it was the planned work. Scope creep at this magnitude isn't accidental, it's a failure to say "no" or "later" in real time.

Exit criteria: Every unplanned item is P0-justified, explicitly traded, or moved out.

3. Review column blitz KEEP

▼
Open the Review column.
For each PR > 24 hours old without your review comment: review or close. No exceptions.
If you can't merge today, leave a "next action" comment so the author isn't blocked guessing.
gh pr list --repo konvoy-kegs/konvoy --state open \ --json number,title,author,createdAt,reviewDecision \ | jq '.[] | select(.reviewDecision != "APPROVED")'
Exit criteria: Every Review-column PR has an action comment from today, or is merged.

4. CI / infra spot-check KEEP

▼
GitHub Actions: any failing scheduled workflows on main?
Dokploy / EKS: any pods in CrashLoopBackOff or unschedulable?
LaunchDarkly: any flags at 100% rollout for > 14 days that should be removed?
Exit criteria: No silent red on main, no zombie flags.

5. Day 8 scope-freeze warning KEEP

▼
On the second Wednesday of the sprint, post a scope-freeze reminder: "Scope freezes Friday EOD. Anything not Ready by then rolls to next sprint."
Exit criteria: Team knows freeze is coming.
Goal: ship what can ship, document what won't, set up Monday so it isn't a fire drill. ≈45 min. On end-of-sprint Fridays, run the Sprint Close-Out tab after this one.

1. Review blitz KEEP

▼
60-minute focused block, early in the day (reviewing brain works best fresh).
Goal: Review column ≤ 5 by EOD.
Anything that can't be reviewed: leave a comment with the date you'll get to it.
Exit criteria: Review ≤ 5, or a written commitment on each remaining item.

2. Done audit KEEP

▼
Walk the Done column. For each item: is it actually merged + deployed + verified, or just code-complete?
Anything code-complete-but-not-deployed: move back to QA or Review.
Why this matters most

This is the single biggest source of "we said it was done and it wasn't" pain. Tighten DoD once — merged + deployed + smoke-tested + Sentry clean for 24h — and the entire downstream signal cleans up. Loose DoD makes every other metric a lie.

Exit criteria: Done means done.

3. Sentry triage DELEGATE → on-call

▼
Sentry: any unresolved P1 from the week. File or assign.
Cross-check resolved Sentry items against deployed tickets — pattern-spot for recurring root causes.
Exit criteria: No P1 Sentry issue without an owner.

4. Friday async wrap KEEP

▼
Post a 5-line #eng-katch-cloud summary: shipped this week, in review, blocked, top priority for Monday.
Tag the PM/COO if anything they care about slipped.
Exit criteria: Team and stakeholders end the week with a shared picture.

5. Calendar pre-load KEEP

▼
Confirm Monday's standup post template is ready (copy from last week).
If next week is sprint week 1, confirm sprint planning is on the calendar.
Exit criteria: Monday starts on rails.
Goal: retro inputs ready, next sprint scoped, dashboard published. Run on the final Friday of a sprint, after the standard Friday block.

1. Generate the sprint review dashboard KEEP

▼
Create sprint-review-YYYY-MM-DD.html in engineering-planning/ using the dark-theme template.
Pull numbers from Insights: items shipped, items carried, points completed, review peak.
Add retro structure: went well / review / process questions.
Update index.html: add a new card at the top, move the "Latest" badge.
git -C engineering-planning add . git -C engineering-planning commit -m "Add sprint review YYYY-MM-DD" git -C engineering-planning push origin main
Exit criteria: New review live at the published site.

2. Next sprint scoping KEEP

▼
Identify 3 sprint goals — no more. Pre-agree with CEO/COO.
Allocate Ready items to people, respecting ~22 SP sustainable velocity per dev (and your own — historically you've been over by 60%).
Leave a 20% buffer. Sprints planned to 100% land at 60%.
Exit criteria: Each dev has a named milestone allocation, total ≤ velocity, buffer reserved.

3. Retro KEEP — facilitator

▼
Run the retro from the dashboard as the visual aid.
Focus on team-level themes, not individual output. At 3 devs individual metrics are noise; at 30 they're toxic.
Capture 2–3 action items with named owners and due dates. Anything else is wishful thinking.
Exit criteria: Retro doc updated with action items, owners, due dates.
Run this self-check at the end of each Friday block. If you can't tick all of these, something earlier in the week was skipped.

End-of-week verification

▼
Every In-Progress item has an assignee and a size.
Review column ≤ 5.
Each sprint goal has a named next ticket.
No blocker is unowned.
No untriaged P1/P2 Sentry from the last 7 days.
Monday's standup post template is ready.

Why a hygiene cadence at all?

Sprint planning is one of those things where the cost of skipping is invisible until it's catastrophic. You can run a sprint with no hygiene and it'll feel fine. By sprint 3 you have 14 items in Review (Sprint 2's actual peak), 80 SP of unplanned work pulled in mid-sprint (also actual), and a milestone tracking at 8% with the sprint already half over. None of those failures looked dangerous on the day they started. They all looked like "I'll get to it tomorrow."

A cadence isn't there because the work changes daily. It's there because the cost of letting drift compound is exponential, and you need forced checkpoints before the drift is irreversible.

Why Monday / Wednesday / Friday specifically?

Three checkpoints per week is the smallest cadence that catches all three failure modes:

  • Monday catches start-of-week drift: weekend work merged without your review, things that "felt urgent" Friday but aren't, weekend Sentry alerts that need triaging before Monday's deploys.
  • Wednesday catches mid-sprint scope creep and review bottleneck while there's still time to fix it. By Thursday you're committed. Wednesday is the last day you can drop a sprint goal without it being a public failure.
  • Friday catches end-of-week reality: what shipped vs what we said would ship, what to communicate, and — critically — sets up Monday so it's not a fire drill.

Daily would be too noisy. Weekly is too slow — by the time Friday tells you Wednesday went sideways, you've lost the sprint.

Why these specific steps?

Each step is a scar from a specific failure. The ones to internalise:

  • Walk the board right-to-left — left-to-right is how work flows, right-to-left is how problems are discovered. State lies at the end of the pipeline.
  • Review column ≤ 5 — the leading indicator of every other failure. Above 5, devs context-switch to chase reviewers, PRs age, conflicts pile up, next sprint inherits the mess.
  • Sustainable velocity (~22 SP/dev) — not what you can do in a heroic sprint, what you can do every sprint without burning people out. Rob personally entered Sprint 3 at 35+ SP. That number is the bug.
  • 20% buffer — every sprint will have a P0 bug, an outage, or a stakeholder ask you didn't see coming. Plan to 80% so the unplanned work is absorbed instead of pushing planned work out.
  • Goals at the team level, not the individual — at 3 devs the signal-to-noise of individual metrics is terrible. At 30 it's actively destructive: ticket-shopping, undermined pairing, punishing helpers. Build the team-level muscle now.
  • Done means merged + deployed + verified — anything looser and "done" becomes "I think it's done" becomes "we shipped it last sprint but it's not working."

Why a runbook for a 3-person team?

First, it turns memory-dependent process into reproducible process. Right now the process lives in your head. If you take a week off, it stops. If you get a fourth dev, you re-teach it from scratch. A written runbook is the difference between "Rob runs sprint planning" and "Katch Cloud runs sprint planning, and Rob facilitates it."

Second, the habits that work at 3 are the habits that work at 30. The steps don't change — what changes is who runs them. Today you do all of it. At 2 teams, Monday board reset becomes a 15-min team-lead sync. At 5 teams, Friday close-out becomes a 30-min EM ops meeting. Same checkpoints, same questions, different rooms. If you wait until you have 5 teams to figure out what the hygiene questions are, you'll spend a year reinventing them.

The work you're doing now isn't a small-team workaround. It's the prototype of how you'll run engineering at scale.

Anti-patterns to watch for

  • The runbook becomes a checkbox exercise. Steps get ticked without the underlying check actually happening. Fix: every quarter, re-read this appendix. If a step feels like noise, either change it or kill it.
  • You skip "just this week" because you're slammed. Most common failure mode. The week you're most slammed is the week you most need the cadence.
  • You add more steps over time. Sprint hygiene runbooks die from bloat. Keep the daily total under 90 minutes/week. If you add a step, kill a step.
  • You make the runbook precious. It's a living doc. Edit it. Mark it up. The goal is a working operating system, not a beautiful artifact.
