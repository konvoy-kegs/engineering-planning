Milestone 35 Review & M36 Plan

Sprint ran 3 – 16 November 2026 · Next sprint M36, 17 – 30 November · Team of 3 · Prepared for the CEO & COO · 17 November 2026

A lot reached the main branch; less reached customers. PHP 8.5 landed on the main branch on 9 November and its epic closed at 22 of 22. View as User, which lets staff see an organisation exactly as one of its members does, read-only, landed on 12 November. Seven fixes for the November list, including four security fixes and both pricing fixes, all have an approved pull request. None of this is in production yet: the last release was v1.91.2 on 7 November.

The numbers held up. Milestone 35 closed at 74% (66 of 89), against 79% last time. Behind it, 78 issues closed and 50 pull requests merged; 18 of the closures were the backlog triage on 16 November. Millicent merged the most (21), mostly the PHP quality-tooling work; Bea took the customer dashboard to 17 of 21 across two releases. No infrastructure changes were merged.

What did not move: Kubernetes, and the customer Billing switch. Kubernetes 1.35 now has 15 days to the 2 December deadline and no status change since 26 October. Customer Billing is still switched off, waiting on Finance. The plan for M36, in order: merge the seven fixes and release them with PHP 8.5 and View as User; build the holiday lock, whose warning must be live by 8 December; then the Ready list. Kubernetes needs a yes or a no today.

Released · v1.91.1 and v1.91.2 · 5 and 7 November

Two small releases, both mostly the customer dashboard. v1.91.1 on 5 November carried five dashboard pieces and four fixes. v1.91.2 on 7 November carried three customer Rentals refinements and two fixes.

Live now: the overdue-invoices tile, the rates card, the provisional pill on the contract band, the Forecast page’s missing-months banner and the v2.2 customer Rentals page, all behind the dashboard switch; order-confirmation emails on by default for customers who sign up; the Rentals download keeps the transport location; a service-worker failure no longer raises an error; casual agreements can no longer be extended. Waiting on the main branch for the next release: PHP 8.5, View as User and its one-tab fix, and 14 commits in all since v1.91.2.

What we finished

Six themes, in the order a manager should care about them. Underlined names link to the GitHub record. “Live” means in production; “Switched off” means in production but dark behind its switch; “On main” means merged and waiting for the next release.

1. The November list: seven fixes approved, none merged Approved

Of the 16 tickets the outstanding-work review suggested finishing by 30 November, 2 are closed and 7 have a pull request that Millicent has approved: the four security fixes, both pricing fixes and the fix for the consumer breaker that caused the 5 November incident. Each still has one to three review comments to resolve before it can merge. The other 7 need a person or a decision. Details are on the outstanding-work review.

Why it matters: every security code fix on the list is written and approved; what is left is resolving comments, merging and releasing.

2. PHP 8.5 reached the main branch On main

The upgrade that finished on its branch last sprint merged to the main branch on 9 November (the PHP 8.5 epic, closed at 22 of 22). The last report planned to release it on its own so any fault would be unmistakably the runtime. It has not been released yet, and View as User and the seven fixes are now queued behind it.

Why it matters: the runtime upgrade is done in code; the decision now is how to release it.

3. The customer dashboard: 12 of 19 to 17 of 21 Switched off

Bea’s sprint again. The overdue-invoices tile, the rates card, the provisional contract pill, the Forecast missing-months banner and the v2.2 customer Rentals page all shipped in v1.91.1; the Rentals defaults (rolling twelve months, newest first, clearer column names) followed in v1.91.2 (the customer portal epic). The epic now has 21 pieces, up from 19. The dashboard’s production switch is still off.

Why it matters: the dashboard is nearly complete, which is why the sign-up and roles work below now matters: more customers will log in once it opens.

4. View as User: see an organisation as one of its members On main

Staff can now preview an organisation exactly as a chosen member sees it, and the preview is read-only: any attempt to change something is refused by the API, not just hidden in the screen (read-only member preview). It stays in one tab with a way back, the Billing page can be previewed too, and the user guide is written. Merged 9–13 November.

Why it matters: support can answer “what does this customer see?” without asking for a screenshot, and without any risk of changing the customer’s data.

5. PHP quality tooling: the same checks on every machine Branch

Millicent’s sprint. The PHP checks now run before every commit, fast enough not to be skipped; PHPStan and Rector were upgraded in one step; one formatter replaced two; PhpStorm is set up with the same rules as CI; and CI reports code complexity on changed methods. The epic is at 12 of 14 (the PHP quality-tooling epic); the last two pull requests are waiting on Rob’s review.

Why it matters: problems the CI gate used to find after a push are now caught before the commit.

6. The backlog triage and the order-email investigation Done

On 16–17 November every open 2026 ticket and unstarted EPIC was checked against merged code and the board: 20 closed, 46 put on hold pending the finance-integration decision (44 after two were taken off on 17 November), and 23 marked Ready for this sprint (the triage outcome). Separately, Rob’s investigation into how order emails to warehouses are sent and confirmed closed 9 of its 18 pieces (the order-email epic).

Why it matters: the sprint starts from a board that says what is ready and what is parked.

Five things to watch

Ordered by how quickly they need a decision.

1. Kubernetes 1.35 — 15 days, four sprints of no movement

Standard support for the current version ends on 2 December. The last report put the surcharge after that at $2,147 AUD a month until all four clusters are upgraded. There has been no status change on the upgrade board since 26 October.

Decision: yes means it goes ahead of rows 3 to 5 this sprint; no means budgeting the surcharge from December. One or the other today.

2. Two access findings have no owner

Of the four access findings in the last report, one has an approved fix waiting to merge and one needs an owner’s keep-or-revoke decision. The other two have no owner and are not on the sprint board. Details stay off this page until they are fixed.

What would help: name an owner for each and put both in M36, or record that they wait.

3. Customer Billing is still switched off

The last report expected customers on Billing around 16–17 November. The switch is still off in production. The legal-entity wording has no Finance owner, and how currency names map to codes is still being decided.

What would help: a named Finance owner for the wording, and a date to aim for.

4. The holiday lock’s warning must be live by 8 December

That is three weeks away, on the Tuesday of M37’s second week, and it has to be released by then. Last year’s notice was on the order form only.

What would help: settle today which markets, whether staff are exempt, and who approves the customer wording.

5. A large release is building up

PHP 8.5, View as User and, once merged, seven fixes including two that change billed amounts. The last report’s plan was to release PHP 8.5 on its own.

What would help: agree today whether PHP 8.5 goes out first on its own, or with the fixes.
