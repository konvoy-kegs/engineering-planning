Milestone 31 Review & Sprint Plan

Sprint ran 31 August – 11 September 2026 · Next sprint 14 – 25 September · Team of 3 · Prepared for the CEO & COO · 14 September 2026

A lot of work was finished this fortnight, and none of it reached customers. Across all milestones the team closed 48 issues and merged 42 pull requests — about the same output as the last sprint. But no production release has been published since v1.90.0 on 31 August. The next one, v1.90.1, has sat as a draft the whole time. Everything below that says “shipped” means merged and ready, not live.

The milestone figure looks worse than the fortnight was. Milestone 31 closed at 56% (34 of 61 items), down from 78%, with 27 items carried. That is partly real — two revenue defects were carried for the third time — and partly bookkeeping: more than half of the issues closed this fortnight sat in other milestones or in none, so the sprint milestone never saw them.

Two things need a decision today. First, publish the release. Second, on 3 September eleven commits reached the main branch without a pull request or a review — caught and reverted within five hours, re-landed properly a week later, no harm done. But the branch rules let it happen, and the AI-governance audit completed at the weekend says the same thing from a different angle.

Not shipped

No release reached production during the sprint. v1.90.0 went out on 31 August, the day the sprint started, and nothing has followed. v1.90.1 exists only as an unpublished draft. Last report’s first watch item asked for a maximum age on a cut-but-unpublished release; it was not set, and this sprint is the result — two weeks of reviewed, merged work, including six security fixes and the refill-billing guard, waiting in front of nobody.

The infrastructure release debt is unchanged: kubernetes #475 (develop → main reconcile) has now not been touched since 19 June — nearly three months, and its fourth consecutive report.

What we finished

Seven themes, in the order a manager should care about them. “Merged” means done and reviewed but not yet in production — see the release note above.

1. Billing in Konvoy Cloud went from plumbing to screens In progress

Customers and staff will soon see invoices and credit notes inside Konvoy Cloud instead of emailing Finance. This sprint delivered the first-pass Billing page — list, filters and empty/error states (#5842) — and solved the awkward question of which Konvoy entity an invoice belongs to when a customer trades with both Konvoy Australia and Konvoy Services (#5838, #5841, #6095). The code that fetches the PDF from NetSuite is written; it is waiting on a small NetSuite-side deployment (#6047).

The work also turned up a finance defect nobody had asked about: Konvoy Services invoices are printing from the wrong NetSuite template, and the earlier fix covered 57 of 2,370 documents (#6119).

Why it matters: this is the feature that removes a recurring category of Finance email. Its scope milestone is due 30 September with 16 items still open, including the design review with Finance — that date is at risk unless the review happens this fortnight.

2. Refill orders can no longer bill at $0 Merged

The defect first raised in July (#5461) finally has its code fix: any refill or venue-collection order whose fee would resolve to $0 is now rejected, both when it is created and when it is completed (#5458). The Molly Rose order that started it was corrected to the right rate (#5457).

What remains is data and a Finance decision, not code: 16 pricing rows across 8 exclusive agreements still hold a seeded zero and need confirmed rates from Kim, and one completed $0 order needs a credit-or-rebill call.

Why it matters: recurrence is blocked once this is released. The 16 rows are the money already at risk, and they need an owner in Finance, not Engineering.

3. Six security fixes, none of them from an incident Merged

A customer using the “View as User” preview reported seeing admin menus they should not have (#5891) — fixed. Credentials could appear in error stack traces, and the login handshake used a weaker random number than it should (#5677) — fixed. Single-use sign-in tokens were travelling in the page address and so reaching our analytics provider (#6045) — moved to a header. The sign-up flow gained the missing checks on its write paths (#5984), a Content-Security-Policy is now in place in report-only mode (#5686), and Google Tag Manager was removed from the app altogether.

Why it matters: each one shrinks what a third-party script, an analytics vendor or a curious customer could see. None was a breach; all of them are the kind of finding that would have been a breach eventually. None is live until v1.90.1 ships.

4. The Archive and exclusivity work is closed out Shipped in v1.90.0

The completed Archive action shipped on 31 August (#5991) and the user guide was corrected the same day so it stopped describing behaviour that no longer existed (#5999). The “one bad row takes the customer list down” defect finished its second, database-side release (#5993). The business’s request to date-stamp and confirm the end of an extended contract (#6026) was built and merged — after a detour described in the watch list.

Still to run in production: the clean-up of 32 already-archived customers still carrying agreements and 15 still flagged exclusive (#6001). The script is merged; the run is pending.

Why it matters: this closes the loop on Patrick’s housekeeping question from August. Archive now does what it says, and the leftovers from before the fix are one script run from clean.

5. SonarQube is out; our own quality gate is in, and teething Cutover pending

The SonarCloud scan steps were removed from the build on 2 September and the original replacement epic was closed with its eight sub-issues (#5768). The new gate learned to flag only what a pull request actually introduced rather than blaming it for pre-existing duplication (#6058), and three dependency advisories that were turning it red were resolved.

The honest part: six of this fortnight’s issues were defects in the gate itself — an unreachable audit registry failing every frontend PR, a package registry that stopped answering, a duplication check blaming the wrong side of the diff. Four are fixed; two are open. And the step that makes the gate required on the main branch (#5870) is still not done — it has now been open for four weeks.

Why it matters: owning the tool means owning its bugs; a fortnight of teething is normal, a second would not be. Whether the SonarCloud subscription has actually been cancelled is a question for the invoice, not the board.

6. Three feature flags retired, one clean-up reversed Merged

The location-metrics permission flag (#5918) and the orders from/to-location filter flag (#5919) were retired with their features on for everyone. The Databricks asset-cycle dashboard was fenced off behind its flag pending the Databricks decommission (#5926). Map node-clustering scaffolding was removed on 3 September (#5978) and put back behind a flag on 11 September — a small flip-flop worth noting only because it cost two reviews.

Why it matters: every retired flag is one less switch that can be set wrong in production and one less line on the LaunchDarkly bill.

7. Small things support will notice Merged

Order-number search now works without the type prefix, answering a support request (#6032). Empty result pages stopped offering a “next page” that could not exist (#6103). Sentry alerts are now scoped to genuine errors, as code (#5710). And the Dev-OC feedback bridge epic, in flight since June, finally closed (#5422).

Why it matters: fewer “the search doesn’t find my order” tickets, and an alert feed the team will keep reading.

Seven things to watch

Ordered by how quickly they need a decision.

1. Two weeks of finished work has not shipped

Forty-two pull requests merged; zero releases published. Six security fixes and the refill-billing guard are among the work sitting in the v1.90.1 draft. This was watch item 1 last time as a ten-day gap; it is now a full sprint.

Decision: publish v1.90.1 this week, and adopt the rule proposed last time — a cut release is published within two working days or someone says why not.

2. Eleven commits reached the main branch without review

On 3 September a feature branch was created tracking the remote main branch, so a routine push landed eleven commits directly on main with no pull request, no automated checks and no reviewer (#6034). The work itself was sound and had passed every local check; it was reverted within five hours and re-landed through a proper review a week later. No customer impact. The point is that the branch protection allowed it. The AI-governance audit, independently, found that only one status check is actually required on main.

Decision: remove the administrator bypass on the main-branch ruleset and make the test and quality checks required, so this cannot happen by accident to anyone — human or AI assistant.

3. The lease-fee defect has been carried three times Open 25 days

Editing customer pricing silently clears the lease fees on the same row (#5889). Last report asked for it at the top of Milestone 31. It was in Milestone 31; it was not done. It is a revenue defect of the same silent kind as the refill $0 problem, which took three sprints to fix.

Decision: first item in the new sprint with a named owner, or an explicit statement that it is accepted until the Billing epic replaces the screen.

4. The Billing milestone’s 30 September date is at risk

Sixteen items open, and the two that gate the rest are not engineering work: a design review with Finance and management that has not been scheduled, and a NetSuite deployment. Good progress this sprint does not change that arithmetic.

Decision: book the Finance design review this week, or move the milestone date now rather than discover it on the 30th.

5. Second-account identity bug, third sprint Open 42 days

#5647: signing into a second account keeps the first user’s identity. It was flagged last time as belonging with the access-control work. It was placed in Milestone 31 and carried again.

Decision: schedule it or park it explicitly. It should not appear in a fourth report unchanged.

6. Half of the closed work is invisible to the sprint milestone

21 of the 48 issues closed this fortnight had no milestone; a further 13 were in scope milestones. The completion percentage is therefore measuring a shrinking slice of what the team does. Sprint milestones have now formally retired in favour of the board’s Sprint field, which should fix this — provided items are actually placed in it.

Nothing from you — flagging that the headline percentage will change meaning from next report, and that the Sprint field, not a milestone, will be its source.

7. The infrastructure release debt is now three months old

kubernetes #475 — the develop-to-main reconcile — has not been touched since 19 June. Fourth consecutive report. No kubernetes changes merged this sprint, so at least the gap did not widen.

Decision: the same as last time — an owner and a date, or close it and re-cut the branch from current state.
